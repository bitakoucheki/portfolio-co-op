01 · Learning Goals

S.M.A.R.T. Goals — Co-op Term 2026

Three goals for the term, each tied to a York co-op competency. Written at the start of the placement and evaluated on measurable milestones.

Goal 01 of 03

Build a habit of structured problem-solving.

Critical Thinking & Problem Solving

By August 31, 2026, I will strengthen my critical thinking by independently diagnosing and resolving at least three distinct technical problems that arise in my day-to-day data work — whether a pipeline failure, a data quality inconsistency, or an unexpected query result — and for each one, producing a short written post-mortem (one page or less) that documents what the problem was, what I tried, why I chose the resolution I did, and what I would do differently.


Progress will be measured by whether these three documents exist and are substantive enough to share with a peer. The point is not to catalogue every bug I fix, but to force myself to slow down and think analytically about what actually happened and why — something that's easy to skip when you're in the middle of building.


This goal matters because technical problem-solving is not just about finding the fix. It's about developing a mental model of a system well enough to reason about it when things go wrong. Writing that reasoning down is how I make that thinking permanent.

3 documented post-mortems for real technical problems in co-op work

Goal 02 of 03

Deepen analytical fluency — from building to understanding.

Accessing & Analyzing Information

By August 31, 2026, I will demonstrate analytical fluency in two ways: first, by producing thorough technical documentation for at least two data pipelines or transformation workflows I contribute to this term — written clearly enough that a new team member could understand the logic without asking me; second, by completing at least 60% of a supervised machine learning course (Stanford / DeepLearning.AI) and applying one concept from it to describe or analyze something in my actual co-op work in a short written reflection.


Both milestones are measurable by whether the artifact exists: documentation written, course progress tracked, reflection produced. Neither is vague. The goal connects directly to Accessing and Analyzing Information because documentation requires me to articulate not just what a system does but why it is structured the way it is — and the ML course gives me a more formal framework for thinking about what data actually tells us.


Technical execution and analytical fluency are not the same thing. I want to build both, and this goal is designed to push me from doing to understanding.

Goal 03 of 03

Follow curiosity into the spaces between disciplines.

Curiosity & Imagination

By August 31, 2026, I will pursue at least two genuine acts of intellectual curiosity that go beyond what my co-op role requires. The first is a structured conversation — scheduled in advance — with someone at Meridian whose role is meaningfully different from mine (a business analyst, a product manager, a member of the risk team), focused specifically on understanding how they think about data and what questions they most wish they could answer with it. I will write a one-page summary of that conversation immediately after it happens.


The second is a personal project: making at least one meaningful addition to Motif, a generative music engine I built in JavaScript using the Web Audio API. The addition should involve something I have not implemented before — a new compositional rule, a data-driven audio parameter, or an audio-reactive visual element. I will write a short note on what I tried, what surprised me, and what I would want to explore next.


Curiosity is not passive. This goal is designed to make me act on it in ways that are concrete and traceable — a conversation scheduled, a feature shipped, two written reflections that prove it happened. The connection between a generative music system and enterprise data work is not accidental: both are about finding patterns, designing systems with rules, and seeing what emerges. Keeping that cross-disciplinary thinking alive is something I care about and want to protect.

TaskPilot

AI-Assisted Task Management System
A full-stack task management and scheduling platform that pairs calendar-style scheduling with AI-assisted workload balancing. The system automatically detects overloaded schedules, suggests smarter task distribution, and reschedules around timing, priority, and availability.

PostgreSQL Express React Node.js REST APIs Full-Stack

Motif

Live-Coded Music Engine
A browser-based generative music system built around one idea: what if a composition could have logic, not just notes? Motif lets you write music that evolves, reacts, and surprises — driven by conditions and loops rather than a fixed timeline.
Implementation

A custom JavaScript DSL layered on the Web Audio API, with modular audio modules, an event scheduler, and a scrolling piano-roll canvas that visualizes each beat as it triggers.

Outcome

Produced complete tracks end-to-end — from rhythm coding to mastering — showcasing modular audio design, real-time visualization, and the synthesis of an engineering and classical music background.

JavaScript Web Audio API Canvas DSL Design Music Production

Hotel Booking Database

Relational Data System — Course Project
A normalized relational database system for a hotel booking platform, designed from ER diagrams through to optimized, production-style SQL. Built to enforce data integrity and model real-world booking workflows.
Implementation

Modeled rooms, guests, and reservations with enforced referential integrity, then wrote and tuned SQL queries to manage relationships and extract insight from booking data.

Outcome
